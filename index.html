// The Basics

What exactly is "cyber crime"?

Cyber crime is any illegal activity that is carried out using a computer, phone, or the internet. It can target your money, your data, your identity, or even your feelings — and it doesn't need a mask or a weapon, just a screen and a trick.

It's not just a "grown-up problem." Students are frequent targets because they're active on social media, gaming apps, and messaging platforms — often before they've learned to spot the warning signs.

// The Big Picture

Cyber crime falls into three broad categories

Understanding who or what is being targeted helps you spot an attack faster.

01 / AGAINST INDIVIDUALS

Targeting a person

Crimes aimed directly at one person's money, data, dignity or safety.

  • Cyberbullying & harassment
  • OTP / banking fraud
  • Identity theft & impersonation
  • Cyberstalking
02 / AGAINST PROPERTY

Targeting devices & data

Crimes aimed at computers, networks, accounts, or digital assets rather than a person directly.

  • Hacking & unauthorised access
  • Malware, viruses & ransomware
  • Software & content piracy
  • Data theft & breaches
03 / AGAINST SOCIETY

Targeting communities & nations

Larger-scale crimes that threaten public trust, infrastructure, or the wider community.

  • Cyber terrorism
  • Large-scale misinformation
  • Attacks on critical infrastructure
  • Online trafficking networks
// Know Your Enemy

The 8 cyber threats every student should recognise

Cyber criminals rely on one thing above all: you not recognising the trick. Here's what to watch for.

✉

Phishing

Fake emails, texts, or links pretending to be your bank, school, or a well-known brand — designed to steal your passwords or personal details.

Example: "Your account will be blocked, click here to verify" texts.
🔐

OTP & Banking Fraud

Scammers call or message pretending to be bank staff, pressuring you to share the One-Time Password sent to your phone.

Rule: No bank employee will ever ask for your OTP.
💬

Cyberbullying

Using chats, comments, or social media to repeatedly threaten, mock, embarrass, or exclude someone.

Example: Group chats created to insult a classmate.
🪪

Identity Theft

Someone steals your photos, name, or personal details to create fake profiles or pretend to be you online.

Example: A fake Instagram account using your pictures.
🖥

Hacking

Gaining unauthorised access to someone's device, account, or network — often using weak or reused passwords.

Defence: Strong, unique passwords + two-factor login.
🐛

Malware & Viruses

Harmful software hidden in downloads, pirated apps, or attachments that damages your device or steals data quietly.

Example: A "free" cracked game download that hijacks your phone.
🎮

Gaming & App Scams

Fake in-game offers or bonus items that ask you to enter bank details or an OTP to "claim a reward."

Rule: No real reward ever needs your bank OTP.
🏆

Fake Prizes & Lottery Scams

Messages claiming you've "won" a lottery, lucky draw, or gift card — asking for a fee or your details to release it.

Rule: You can't win a contest you never entered.
// How It Actually Happens

Anatomy of a cyber attack

Almost every scam — phishing, OTP fraud, fake prizes — follows the same five-step pattern. Learn the pattern, and you can spot the scam at step one.

1

The Bait

An exciting offer, a scary warning, or a message that looks official — designed purely to grab your attention and short-circuit careful thinking.

2

The Hook

You're pushed to act fast — "offer expires in 10 minutes," "account will be blocked today." Urgency is the attacker's favourite weapon.

3

The Trap

A fake website, form, or app screen — built to look real — asks for your password, card number, or OTP.

4

The Loss

The moment you hand over that detail, the attacker uses it immediately — money is withdrawn, an account is taken over, or data is stolen.

5

The Aftermath

Quick action matters: alerting your bank, changing passwords, and reporting to cybercrime.gov.in or 1930 can limit the damage.

// Case File

A real story: how one in-game "offer" cost a family ₹1.5 lakh

This actually happened — and it shows exactly how these scams are built to fool someone young, curious, and in a hurry.

● Reported Case

The Mobile Game "Bonus" Trap

Who: A 14-year-old student, Lucknow, India Where it started: Inside a popular mobile game Loss: ₹1.5 lakh (his mother's savings) Reported: 2025, local news & cyber cell

A 14-year-old boy was playing a popular mobile game when a pop-up appeared, offering special in-game items and bonus rewards. To "claim" the offer, it asked him to enter bank account details and an OTP.

Caught up in the excitement of the reward, he used his mother's bank details and shared the OTP that arrived on her phone — not realising that a real bank or game would never need that code. Within minutes, ₹1.5 lakh vanished from the family's account. His mother noticed the deduction message almost immediately and filed a complaint with the bank and the local cyber crime cell, and police began investigating the fraud.

WHY THIS TRICK WORKED
  • The offer felt exciting and time-limited, so he acted fast without thinking.
  • It looked like part of the game, not like a "scam."
  • He didn't know that an OTP should never be entered for a free reward.
WHAT TO DO INSTEAD
  • Never enter bank details or OTPs inside a game or app for a "bonus."
  • If something asks for money-related info to give you something free — stop and tell an adult.
  • Check bank SMS alerts immediately and report suspicious deductions right away.

Based on news reports of a cyber fraud case investigated by Lucknow police, 2025. Details are summarised for educational use.

// Your Defence Kit

Simple habits that stop most cyber crime

You don't need to be a tech expert — just a few smart habits, used consistently.

✔ Do this

  • Use strong, unique passwords with letters, numbers & symbols for every account.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it's offered.
  • Think before you click — hover over or check a link before opening it.
  • Keep your profiles private and only accept requests from people you actually know.
  • Tell a parent or teacher immediately if something online feels wrong.
  • Download apps only from official app stores.
  • Log out of shared or public devices after use.
  • Update your apps and phone software regularly.

✘ Avoid this

  • Never share your OTP, password, or PIN with anyone — ever, for any reason.
  • Don't click links from unknown numbers, emails, or pop-ups promising prizes.
  • Don't share your address, school name, or live location with online strangers.
  • Don't use the same password on multiple sites.
  • Don't do banking or shopping on public/free Wi-Fi.
  • Don't download apps or files from unofficial links sent on chat.
  • Don't respond to threats or blackmail online — save evidence and report instead.
  • Don't believe "too good to be true" offers — because they usually are.

Something feels wrong online? Report it.

In India, cyber crime can be reported for free, anytime, through the official government portal or helpline.
